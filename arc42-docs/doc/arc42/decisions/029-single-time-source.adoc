[[adr-029]]
= ADR-029: A Single Injectable Time Source, Two Readings of One Moment

== Status
Accepted, implemented.

== Context
Calendar dates and timestamps were read through roughly seventy direct `LocalDate.now()` /
`OffsetDateTime.now()` calls scattered across the codebase. Each call silently depended on the
container's `TZ` setting rather than on a product property, and the yearly reset of number ranges
— an invoice number carries the year it was issued in — could not be tested deterministically: a
test could not fix "now" at the year boundary without controlling every call site individually.
A `java.time.Clock` bean already existed, but only five services actually used it.

== Decision
One component, `TimeSource` (`de.littletrotter.shared.time`, `kotlin-shared`), replaces every
direct `now()` call in every module. `ClockTimeSource` implements it over a `java.time.Clock`.
`kotlin-shared` has no CDI, so each deployable produces its own `@ApplicationScoped` instance over
the system UTC clock: `TimeSourceProducer` in `kotlin-invoice`, a producer of its own in
`kotlin-xpdf`. `kotlin-facturx` has no CDI either and receives the moment from its caller as a
parameter.

`TimeSource` exposes two readings of the same instant, not one:

* *Points in time* — `instant()`, `nowUtc()` — are UTC. Everything stored, compared, or measured
  uses these.
* *Calendar dates* — `today()`, `localNow()` — are read in the business zone, `Europe/Berlin`
  (`GermanTime.ZONE`, `kotlin-shared`), the zone the user actually works in.

The distinction matters at midnight: an invoice sent at 00:30 on 1 January in Berlin belongs to
the new year, although UTC still reads 31 December.

A stored moment becomes a calendar day only through `GermanTime.dayOf(...)` (`kotlin-shared`), never
through the moment's own offset (`OffsetDateTime.toLocalDate()` on a UTC value) or the process zone
(`ZoneId.systemDefault()`). The offer's day of sending, for example, is the German day of the
actor's UTC timestamp, so an offer dated today can be sent at 00:30.

Invoice numbers are drawn for the invoice's own business date, not for the instant the allocation
happens: `InvoiceNumberService.generateNextNumber(sender, date, …)` takes `invoice.invoiceDate`,
which is stamped once at the invoice's `→ SENT` transition. The number's embedded year and the
printed invoice date can therefore never diverge across a midnight boundary.

Persistence and transport stay UTC throughout: every database timestamp column is `TIMESTAMPTZ`,
Hibernate reads and writes UTC (`quarkus.hibernate-orm.jdbc.timezone=UTC`), event stream envelopes
and payloads (NATS JetStream) carry instants in UTC, and object storage metadata (RustFS) adheres
strictly to UTC. The UI renders timestamps in the user's local time (DST-aware, winter/summer time),
defaulting to `Europe/Berlin` (`GermanTime.ZONE`), and paving the way for multi-timezone operations
across distributed branches for mid-sized enterprises. Entity audit stamps
(`AuditableEntity.createdAt`/`updatedAt`) are written by `AuditTimestampListener`, creation moments of
the remaining entities by `CreationStampListener` (`StampedOnCreation`); both are JPA entity listeners
that Quarkus runs as CDI beans — so the domain itself never needs a clock for its own bookkeeping, and
Hibernate's generated timestamps are not used at all.

The database does not invent time either. The schema has no `DEFAULT NOW()`/`CURRENT_DATE`; every
writer supplies its dates and timestamps. The invoice audit trigger stamps `occurred_at` from the
session setting `app.now` instead of `clock_timestamp()` and rejects a write without it.
`AppNowConnectionInterceptor` (an Agroal pool interceptor) sets `app.now` from the `TimeSource` on
every connection the application takes from the pool; any other writer — an admin script, the
`datagen` bulk loader — sets it itself. Native SQL binds timestamps as parameters instead of calling
`CURRENT_TIMESTAMP`; seed rows carry the fixed baseline moment. Domain methods that need a date or
instant take it as a parameter instead of reading the clock themselves.

== Enforcement
ArchUnit forbids in production code any `now(...)` call on a `java.time` type, any `Clock.system*`
factory call, `System.currentTimeMillis()`, `System.nanoTime()`, building a `ClockTimeSource` or a
fixed/offset/ticking `Clock`, and Hibernate's `@CreationTimestamp`/`@UpdateTimestamp`/
`@CurrentTimestamp`, outside `ClockTimeSource` and the module's own producer. It also forbids
`OffsetDateTime.toLocalDate()` and `ZoneId.systemDefault()` outside `GermanTime`, so calendar days
are read in German time only. The rules exist once, in `TimeRules` of `kotlin-architecture-rules`
(<<adr-038,ADR-038>>), self-tested there against violating and permitted specimens; every module
(`kotlin-shared`, `kotlin-facturx`, `kotlin-xpdf`, `kotlin-invoice`, the offer and mail modules)
checks its own production classes with them in `TimeSourceArchitectureTest`.

== Testability
Unit tests fix time with `ClockTimeSource.fixedAt(instant)` or `fixedAtLocal(localDateTime)`, or a
mock. Quarkus tests swap the application-scoped bean per test with
`QuarkusMock.installMockForType`. A year-rollover test fixes the clock at 31 December 23:59:59 and
at 1 January 00:00, both Berlin time, and asserts the numbering and date behavior on each side of
the boundary.

== Alternatives Considered
* *A static/ambient clock holder:* rejected — global mutable state invites test cross-talk between
  parallel test classes and hides the dependency from a bean's constructor.
* *Injecting raw `java.time.Clock` everywhere:* rejected — every call site would then choose its
  own zone conversion independently, reintroducing the inconsistency this decision removes.
* *Relying on the container's `TZ=Europe/Berlin`:* rejected — a deployment setting is not a product
  property; the business zone must not depend on how a given container happens to be configured.

== Consequences

=== Positive
* Year- and month-boundary behavior (number ranges, reporting cutoffs) is deterministically
  testable by fixing one clock.
* Business logic no longer depends on the container's `TZ` configuration.
* The business zone is defined in exactly one place and can be changed there alone.

=== Negative
* Every bean that needs "now" takes a `TimeSource` constructor parameter instead of calling a
  static method — more constructor wiring throughout the codebase.
* Every writer outside the application — admin scripts, `datagen` — must set `app.now` before it
  touches a journaled table; a forgotten setting fails the write instead of silently stamping the
  database's clock.
* Elapsed-time measurements use the wall clock of the `TimeSource` rather than a monotonic counter;
  a clock step during a measurement skews that one log value, never a stored business value.
